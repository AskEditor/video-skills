# The story

Find and read the sources before you write a word. Keep each fact with its two quotes.

## The test

A story passes when all four are yes.

| Ask | A yes looks like |
|---|---|
| Does one person get the better of somebody stronger (richer, armed, in office, or many against one), or do the strong make fools of themselves? | A shoemaker arrests a mayor. A government pays for dead snakes |
| Can the first event be said in one line, with no name or date? | Somebody puts on a uniform that is not theirs |
| Is there one fact to keep for the last line, one that turns everything before it? | You choose it, and keep it back |
| Are there two sources a line? | A trial report, and a historian who read the court files |

If one is no, say which, and offer others. A story that is only sad, or only grim, is not one for this style. Say so kindly and find three
that pass.

## No story given?

Find three that pass, from different centuries and countries. Show each in three lines: who, what they dared, the last line. Recommend one.

## Told already?

Look for it on YouTube Shorts four ways: the main person's name, the event, the place and the year, and the event as a question. Write
down how many different Shorts you found and the biggest view count. If a very big one tells it already, ask the person before going on.
If you cannot search YouTube, say so and ask them to look.

## STOP

Show what happens in five lines, the sources, the two numbers, and what you left out and why.
