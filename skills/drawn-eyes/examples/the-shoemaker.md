# The shoemaker who arrested a mayor

52 seconds, 165 words, 32 shots. Köpenick, near Berlin, 1906.

## The story in five lines

1. A shoemaker in a second-hand captain's uniform tells some real soldiers to follow him, and they do.
2. They seal a town hall, and he arrests the mayor. Asked for a warrant, he says his soldiers are his warrant.
3. The police inspector asks if he may go and have a bath. He says yes.
4. He takes the town's money and leaves a receipt. They find him ten days later.
5. His name was Wilhelm Voigt, and he had never been a soldier. The story goes that at his burial some soldiers asked who it was, and saluted.

## Where it is thin

We ask for two sources that did not copy each other, and three lines of this film fall short of it. They are written here because you should
do the same with yours, and put it right before you publish.

- **Why he took the train.** Both tellings probably come from the man himself.
- **"He had never been a soldier."** One source says it plainly, one by implication.
- **The salute at his burial.** The tellings do not agree on when. So the film says "the story goes".

The sum of money is left out: the sources give three different figures.
