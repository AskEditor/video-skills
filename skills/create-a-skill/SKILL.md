# Create a skill from a video

You take one kind of video and write down how it is made, so that an agent who has never seen it can make another. What you take away is the
craft. Never the pictures, the people, the words, the voice, the music, the names or the story.

## 1. Whose is it?

Ask. Work only from a file the person hands over, one they made or have the right to study. Never fetch it yourself. If it is somebody
else's and they have no right to it, say so and offer to work from one of theirs.

## 2. Say what makes it that style

Five lines. Beside them, the list of what will never be copied.

## 3. Read it, don't guess

Look at it several times a second, and at both sides of every cut. Read the time of every spoken word. Then one row a shot:

| From, to | The word it lands on | Who and what | Near or far | What moves | What the face does | Colour | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Nothing from memory. Nothing from one look. One look a second finds the shots and misses most of what changes inside them.

## 4. Count, then check the count

Turn each habit into a number, and write how you counted it. Have a second reader count part of it without seeing yours. Where you differ,
look again. Still differ? Leave it out. Trust a script only after it agrees with a stretch you counted by hand.

## 5. Sort it

- **True in every video you read:** a rule.
- **Different from one to the next:** a choice. Give its range, and when to pick what.
- **Seen in one video only:** write "one video, not a rule" beside it.

## 6. Guess, then look

This needs another video of the same kind. Without one, say so in the skill and skip to 7.

Before you open another video of that style, write down what your rules say it does. Open it, score it, and fix the rules that were wrong.
Check the new rules against the earlier videos. Put the score in the skill, whatever it is.

## 7. Make it yours, and write it for somebody who knows nothing

Change at least four of these, so that nobody could take yours for the one you studied: the people, the places, the type, the colour, the
voice, the story. Then write `SKILL.md` from `TEMPLATE.md`: steps in order, one action a line, a table for every choice made twice, a **STOP** before anything that costs, a table of
what goes wrong, and what it cannot do at the top.

## 8. Prove it, on paper and then for real

A fresh agent reads only the skill, plans twenty seconds, and lists every place it had to guess. Fix, and repeat until nothing stops it. Then
make one. Have a fresh reader list every fault, by shot. Fix one at a time. Report both counts.
